Core Clarification

Multipoles do not convert, replace, or generate an isotropic expansion rate.  
They are a mathematical decomposition that separates an observed expansion field into:

one isotropic piece (the monopole), and  
a hierarchy of anisotropic corrections (dipole, quadrupole, octupole, \ldots).

In the language of spherical harmonics the local expansion rate (or the redshift-distance residual field) is expanded as

\[
H(\hat{n}) = H_0 + \sum_{\ell=1}^{\ell_{\rm max}}\sum_{m=-\ell}^{\ell} a_{\ell m}\, Y_{\ell m}(\hat{n}).
\]

The monopole term (\(\ell=0\)) is precisely the isotropic Hubble constant \(H_0\).  
Every higher multipole quantifies a directional deviation from that isotropic baseline.

Geometric Meaning of Each Order (Standard Interpretation)

| Multipole Order | Geometric Meaning in Cosmic Expansion | Physical Cause / Interpretation |
|-----------------|---------------------------------------|---------------------------------|
| Monopole (\(\ell=0\)) | Isotropic expansion | Uniform baseline cosmic expansion — the standard Hubble constant \(H_0\) that acts equally in all directions |
| Dipole (\(\ell=1\)) | Net directional shift | Bulk flow: the local volume of space is moving collectively toward a preferred direction (e.g., relative to the CMB rest frame) |
| Quadrupole (\(\ell=2\)) | Symmetric stretching / distortion | Gravitational shearing: expansion is slightly faster along one axis and slower along the orthogonal axis because of local large-scale structure |
| Higher orders (\(\ell\geq 3\)) | Complex local fluctuations | Minor localized asymmetries and density inhomogeneities |
